# Error and success register

Generated by `scripts/error_register.py` from a completed forked study, not written by
hand. Regenerate it after every run; the point is that failure modes stop being
anecdotes and start being counts. Current source: 65 held-out Shopify cases,
`gpt-oss-20b` extraction replayed offline, Haiku shopping agent, 9 September 2026.

## What Approv understood the shopper to want

| Attribute | Requests | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| availability | 65 | 100% |
| size | 65 | 100% |
| color | 36 | 55% |
| price | 15 | 23% |
| brand | 1 | 2% |

## What it then said — 175 findings over 65 cases

| Verdict | Count | Dominated by |
|---|---:|---|
| OK | 112 | availability 62, size 22 |
| MISMATCH | 52 | **size 39**, color 12 |
| UNVERIFIED | 11 | color 9 |

Size is where nearly everything happens, on both the request side and the failure side.
A verification layer for this catalogue shape is, in practice, a size checker.

## Detection is not the problem

| The report vs the truth about the agent's pick | Count |
|---|---:|
| Wrong pick, flagged | **38** |
| Wrong pick, reported clean | 2 |
| Right pick, flagged | 2 |
| Right pick, reported clean | 23 |

95% recall at 95% precision.

## Outcomes are the problem

| Arm | Fixed mistakes | Broke successes | Final |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Generic "look again" | 33/40 | 0/25 | **89.2%** |
| Approv report | 18/40 | 2/25 | **63.1%** |

Approv identified 38 of 40 mistakes and converted 18. A control carrying no information
at all converted 33. **The gap is entirely in what the report told the agent to do.**

## By request family

| Family | Baseline | Control | Approv |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| budget | 5/15 | 11/15 | 9/15 |
| clear | 3/10 | 9/10 | 6/10 |
| colour | 5/13 | 12/13 | 9/13 |
| **impossible** (correct answer is to decline) | 3/10 | **10/10** | **5/10** |
| vernacular | 9/17 | 16/17 | 12/17 |

The `impossible` row is the sharpest signal in the table. When nothing in the catalogue
satisfies the request, the control declined every time and Approv declined half the time.
A report that names a mismatch and says to find something satisfying it reads as an
instruction to keep looking, which is exactly wrong when the honest answer is that the
item is not available. **Approv currently has no way to say "stop, and buy nothing."**

## What the control did that Approv should have said

The control corrected 33 mistakes. Reconstructed from the picks:

| Repair move | Count | Approv named the right attribute? |
|---|---:|---|
| Same product, corrected the variant | 17 | yes |
| Switched to a different product | 10 | yes |
| Moved to or from declining | 6 | — |

Approv named the correct attribute in 32 of the 33. It had the diagnosis and gave the
wrong instruction: `PICK_DIFFERENT_PRODUCT` rendered as "Choose a different product",
when in 17 of 33 the fix was to keep the product and take another variant of it.

## Fixes made from this register

- A contradicted nominal value now resolves to `PICK_DIFFERENT_PRODUCT` rather than
  `RESTATE_CLAIM`, which had told the agent to reword the customer's requirement until
  it matched the wrong item.
- The action names its target: "Select an item whose size is 7. That may be another
  variant of the same product, or a different product."
- A satisfaction finding says "the request needs 7" where a grounding finding says
  "claimed 7", so a customer's requirement no longer reads like an agent's assertion.

**None of these are measured yet.** They were derived from this held-out run, so testing
them here would be tuning on the test set. They need a fresh sample.

## Open, unfixed

- No resolution expresses "nothing here satisfies this; decline" — the `impossible` row.
- `UNVERIFIED color` fires 9 times; unclear whether those are real gaps or extraction noise.
- 2 false clears and 2 false alarms are uncharacterised.
